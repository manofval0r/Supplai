# Farid Team-Lead Plan

> **Scope:** Farid owns all non-coding work for Supplai. This includes research, outreach, validation, claims, governance framing, evidence, visual references, report, creative illustration assembly, credits, and submission. Farid does not implement features or conduct code review.

## Product truth to protect

Supplai is an app-first, LGA-authorised emergency exception workflow for ambient tracer commodities. It is demonstrated using clearly labelled fictional facilities, stock, messages, routes, riders, and scenarios. SMS/WhatsApp are simulated intake bridges. The product must never imply a live partnership, medicine movement, carrier integration, price, impact result, or approval that the team has not verified.

## Workstream 1: challenge and AI compliance

## Workstream 2: evidence and local validation

- [ ] Create the claim register. For every external claim, record wording, source URL/document, page or section, date, scope, owner, and status.
- [ ] Audit inherited deck/report claims, especially medicine-availability figures, competitor claims, public-sector numbers, delivery times, and economic assumptions.
- [ ] Conduct at least one short validation call or interview with an LGA health officer, pharmacist, or PHC worker. Get consent before naming anyone.
- [ ] Ask the validation questions: realistic authority chain, ambient tracer commodities, safety-floor rule, batch/expiry fields, source release, receiving inspection, rider incident handling, and LGA escalation path.
- [ ] Turn interview findings into an evidence memo that distinguishes direct feedback, inference, and unresolved assumptions.

## Workstream 3: governance and pilot design

- [ ] Confirm two suitable ambient tracer commodities. Default remains ORS sachets and zinc 20 mg tablets until a credible source changes it.
- [ ] Confirm or qualify the product position: LGA/pharmacist-authorised emergency redistribution, not peer marketplace trading.
- [ ] Review the NAFDAC GSDP and NDPA implications in plain language. Produce a one-page design-control map, not legal advice.
- [ ] Define the single biggest assumption: authorised, usable nearby surplus exists when an urgent request appears.
- [ ] Define the fallback: stockout visibility and escalation into LGA/procurement pathways when safe redistribution is unavailable.
- [ ] Validate each Scenario Lab case for operational realism: authorised transfer, shelf mismatch, safety-floor block, FEFO reroute, delayed rider, refused receipt, and demand shock.
- [ ] Create a transparent pilot-economics worksheet. Label every number as an assumption until externally verified.

## Workstream 4: design and creative direction

- [ ] Collect 6–10 credible UI references for a trustworthy mobile health workflow, notification centre, route/ETA treatment, and focused operations console.
- [ ] Send the co-developers a concise design brief: mood, typography, colour meaning, dashboard density, map/route treatment, notification urgency, motion references, and examples to avoid.
- [ ] Review product screenshots only for claim accuracy, scenario realism, and narrative alignment. Do not conduct code review.
- [ ] Write the three-minute screen-story storyboard: problem, request, source confirmation, authority release, rider handover, receipt, safe failure, pilot close.
- [ ] Maintain captions and narration that clearly call the Scenario Lab fictional/modelled where appropriate.

## Communication rule

Share concise evidence updates with the team: what was verified, source or contact, what it changes, and what remains uncertain. Decisions that change the product scope must be made before the related claim appears in the deck, report, or demo.
